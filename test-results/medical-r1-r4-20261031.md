# Medical R1–R4 live experiment — 31 October 2026

## Executive summary

**The experiment did not answer the medical question.** Five completed search rounds produced 17 unique source records, two adjacent research memories and no synthesis. All five R4 assessments correctly returned `unresolved`. The sixth R1 response violated the five-word concept limit, and the supervisor stopped on that validation failure after **142.666 seconds**, with **49 committed and one rejected controller action**. The requested 35-minute execution allowance and 60-action ceiling were configured, but neither was reached. This is an early blocked run, not a 35-minute research result.

Exact root question:

> Does a spiculated pulmonary lesion apparently doubling in volume over approximately four weeks favour infection over malignancy, and how strongly?

Positive observations include distinct submitted searches throughout this run, durable reuse of an initially unused R1 discovery, and appropriately conservative R4 judgments. These did **not** establish a diagnostic direction, likelihood ratio or probability. More unique records mostly meant more peripheral material. No fixes, alternative terms, supplementary evidence or custom objectives were supplied to Mira.

## Stagnation and weaknesses observed

There is no evidence here of repeated exact searches yielding the same records: every submitted query and returned record was distinct. Nevertheless, **scientific progress stagnated**. Five provider rounds and 17 extraction calls yielded no direct evidence for the root comparison. Two successive R4 assessments had no findings, the next two reused the same single adjacent memory, and both S1 calls declined the same pair.

The strongest observed planning weakness is **query relevance despite lexical novelty**. R1 switched from an overpacked comparator phrase to lists of different diseases that the default PubMed/Europe PMC profiles conjoined with AND. Those disease alternatives became simultaneous retrieval requirements. Round four then combined FAPI, FDG and an inflammatory lesion phrase, shifting attention toward a modality suggested by an incidental uptake study. Its R1 reason described the study as addressing spiculated lesions although the saved finding says irregular borders, and supplied rapid-growth assertions without supporting findings. These are model planning interpretations, not validated scientific discoveries. Novel strings and more unique records did not ensure useful evidence.

Other limitations are the blocking six-word output, inconsistent retention of adjacent PET evidence, repeated synthesis-selection overhead, exact-concept usage flags that omit substring appearances, and source availability dominated by metadata. The strict rejection protected the format contract but ended autonomous execution without a bounded correction path. There was no observed journal corruption, lost provenance, unintended reset, provider retry bypass, fabricated numerical diagnostic answer or false answered state. Scientific validation remains incomplete: structural acceptance did not prevent the significance overstatement in `r1#f1`.

## Recommendations for review only

1. Review a bounded, journaled response-correction policy for invalid R1 concepts. Preserve original responses and prevent repeated identical-call loops; do not silently shorten scientific phrases. No correction was implemented here.
2. Review how scientific alternatives become mandatory AND clauses, and evaluate retrieval relevance with fixed default-profile comparisons. Do not treat terminology diversity or record counts as scientific progress.
3. Review R1's use of explicit evidence gaps so incidental modalities do not displace the selected growth question. Morphology and population qualifications must survive concept selection.
4. Assess source enrichment for metadata-only leads, consistent relevance criteria for R3, and checks against extending statistical significance beyond the source.
5. Clarify exact-concept versus literal-query usage accounting, and consider avoiding redundant S1 evaluation of an unchanged catalogue when no new eligible memory exists.
6. Separately validate search-blocked R4/R5 transitions and synthesis correction under a sustained live workload. This run supplies no live evidence for those paths. Any stagnation policy requires explicit review rather than silently declaring a question answered or exhausted.
