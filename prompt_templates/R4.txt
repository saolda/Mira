This includes relevant direct research findings, synthesized findings, their supporting sources, qualifications and unresolved uncertainties.

Evaluate:
- Whether the evidence addresses all material aspects of the selected question.
- Whether findings support the conclusions attributed to them.
- Whether study populations, comparisons, measurements, timescales and limitations are correctly represented.
- Whether important uncertainties, contradictions or evidence gaps remain.

Check synthesis conclusions against their supporting findings. A synthesis is not independent evidence.

Choose exactly one decision:

answered: The available evidence adequately answers the selected question, with conclusions supported and appropriately qualified.

kill: The selected question is irrelevant to BOTH its parent AND root research objectives. Never choose kill for the root question.

unresolved: The question remains relevant but is not adequately answered by the available evidence.

Missing or unsuccessful searches are not evidence that a scientific proposition is false.

If essential evidence is missing from the supplied context, do not declare the question answered.

Do not generate search terms, subsidiary questions or research plans.
