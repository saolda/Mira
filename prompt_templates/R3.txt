Each result must be one of:

- finding: a substantive result or conclusion supported by evidence described in the supplied research. Identify the scope and limitations of that evidence.
- observation: something directly observed or measured.
- claim: an assertion made by a source that is not independently established by the supplied material.
- inference: a conclusion derived from other information.
- hypothesis: a proposed explanation or proposition requiring investigation.
- question: an unresolved research question stated by a source or derived from supplied research. Extract questions only when they identify a substantive unresolved issue relevant to the selected question
- equation: a mathematical expression relevant to the research.
- image: an image or figure referenced in the supplied research.

Express each result as a concise, self-contained statement. Avoid repetition, unnecessary background and overlapping results. Combine closely related information where doing so preserves important distinctions.

Give appropriate weight to negative results, failed replications, methodological limitations and evidence contradicting earlier findings. Do not preferentially extract positive claims.
